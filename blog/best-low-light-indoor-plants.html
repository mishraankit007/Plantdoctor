10 Best Low-Light Indoor Plants That Are Almost Impossible to Kill

Plant Doctor Team · Plant Care Guides

"Low light" doesn't mean "no light" — every plant on this list still needs some ambient daylight, just not a bright south-facing window. If your space gets indirect light from a north-facing window, or is a few feet back from any window at all, these plants will genuinely thrive rather than just barely survive.

1. Snake Plant (Sansevieria)

The single most forgiving houseplant that exists. Tolerates deep shade, irregular watering, and being ignored for weeks. Water only when the soil is completely dry — roughly every 3–4 weeks indoors.

2. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)

Glossy, architectural leaves that hold up in offices and windowless corners. Stores water in its rhizomes, so it actually prefers you underwater it slightly. Water every 2–3 weeks.

3. Pothos

A fast-growing trailing vine that adapts to almost any light level, including low light — though growth slows and leaves may lose variegation without some brightness. Water when the top inch of soil is dry.

4. Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum)

One of the few low-light plants that flowers indoors. It also tells you exactly when it's thirsty — the leaves droop dramatically, then perk back up within hours of watering.

5. Chinese Evergreen (Aglaonema)

Comes in patterned varieties (silver, pink, red) that add color without needing bright light to keep their markings. Water when the top 1–2 inches of soil are dry.

6. Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra elatior)

Literally named for its toughness. Handles low light, temperature swings, and neglect better than almost anything else on this list. Slow-growing, so patience is the main requirement.

7. Philodendron (heartleaf variety)

A trailing or climbing vine that tolerates low light well, though it grows noticeably faster with a bit more brightness. Water when the top inch of soil dries out.

8. Parlor Palm (Chamaedorea elegans)

A genuinely low-light-tolerant palm (most palms actually need bright light — this is the exception). Adds a lush, tropical look to dim corners. Keep soil lightly moist, never soggy.

9. Maidenhair Vine / Dracaena (several varieties)

Many Dracaena varieties (like Dracaena fragrans or "Janet Craig") handle low light well and grow into architectural, tree-like specimens over time. Water every 1–2 weeks, letting the top dry between waterings.

10. English Ivy

A classic trailing plant that adapts to low light indoors (though it's more demanding than the others on this list about consistent moisture). Great for hanging baskets or shelves away from windows.

A note on "low light"

Every plant here still needs enough ambient light to read a book by during the day — true darkness (a windowless closet, for instance) won't sustain any living plant long-term. If your space is genuinely very dim, a small grow light can make the difference between these plants surviving and actually thriving.
